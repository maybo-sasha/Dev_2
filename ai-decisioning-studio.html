Optimization Power · AI agents pulling in one direction

Agents stop working in silos and begin working toward a shared goal, measuring and optimizing results across complete programs.

  • Group campaigns under one Strategy with a shared KPI goal
  • AI Journey Decisioning, AI Offer Decisioning and Send Time Optimization agents all work together as part of that Strategy (AI Content Decisioning coming soon)
  • Track KPI impact and AI coverage across the whole Strategy

What changed

Give the agents one goal

A Strategy is a goal and the campaigns that serve it. The marketer picks the KPI, adds the campaigns, and the AI agents all work toward that one number together. Before this, each agent optimized its own corner and reported its own result, so nobody could see whether the whole thing was actually working.

01

Pick the goal

The marketer chooses one KPI for the Strategy, deposits or open rate or revenue. Everything inside it is then measured against that single number.

02

Add the campaigns

Campaigns that serve the goal get grouped under the Strategy. The agents can then optimize across all of them at once instead of one campaign at a time.

03

See what the AI added

One score shows how much of the Strategy the agents cover, and the KPI shows what they moved. That is the part marketers never had, and the reason they were willing to switch anything on.

The strategy page

Inside a Strategy

Opening a Strategy lands here. The page shows how it is performing against its KPI over time, the Optimization Score, what each of the four agents is contributing, and every campaign inside it. Optimizing a campaign starts from this table.

The optimization modal

Designing the optimization modal

Turning on an AI agent used to mean leaving the campaign and finding the right screen somewhere else in the product. Most marketers never found it. This modal puts all four agents in one place, opened from the campaign they optimize.

The problem

Nobody could tell what an agent would do

The agents were scattered across the product and their names explained nothing. A marketer could not tell what Journey Decisioning would change about their campaign, so they left it switched off rather than risk finding out.

The solution

Explain first, then guide step by step

The modal opens on the campaign and starts by explaining what the AI has found there and what it is worth in real numbers. From there it walks the marketer through setup one step at a time, so the automation is handed over gradually rather than switched on blind.

The rule

Automation, never a surprise

Each step says what it will change before it changes anything, and nothing is applied until the marketer approves it. Full automation once it runs, full control on the way there.

Journey Decisioning

An AI agent that decides which campaign wins when a customer qualifies for several on the same day.

It replaces the priority rules marketers used to maintain by hand, ranking overlapping journeys on predicted future value. The design problem was orchestration: showing which campaigns competed and why one was chosen, without turning the screen into a rules editor. That screen does not exist yet, and neither does the one that switches the agent on.

Offer Decisioning

An AI agent that finds the right incentive per customer instead of the one that won on average.

A static test picks a single winner and gives everyone the same discount, which quietly costs margin on every customer who would have converted for less. The interface problem is that the saving is invisible: the agent protects margin, and nothing on screen reports how much, which is exactly the number a marketer is asked to justify.

Send Time Optimization

An AI agent that predicts when each customer is most likely to be paying attention.

The simplest of the four to explain and the most awkward to reach. Its setup already existed on a page built before the hub, so the card advertising it points at a route that was never connected. The two screens do not reference each other in either direction. Naming that was most of the contribution here.

Content Decisioning

An AI agent that writes and tests copy variations, then keeps the one that performs.

The only agent designed end to end, and the one the rest of this page follows. It is also the hardest to hand over, because it changes words a marketer wrote, which is why its setup ends in a review rather than an Apply button. It is the only one of the four with a setup screen, a live state and a result a marketer can point at.
