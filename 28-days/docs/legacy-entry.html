Walk the ghetto streets, meet the ŻOB and ŻZW, and follow the preparations and fighting of the uprising. A story of resistance, courage, and people, told through reconstructed scenes and historical evidence. Includes photographs taken during the Holocaust.

19 April 1943 · the first day

Two undergrounds. Two fights.

They fought on the same day, in two places. Both are on the map. Touch one to go there.

Drag to turn the map. Scroll to come closer.

Few of the fighters lived.

Among those who did: Marek Edelman, who stayed in Poland; and Zivia Lubetkin, Yitzhak Zuckerman and Simcha Rotem, who came here. They spent the rest of their lives telling it.

The photographs

All come from Wikimedia Commons. Most are in the public domain; the rest are used under the Creative Commons licence named beside them. Many were taken by the perpetrators, for the Stroop Report. They are shown here as evidence. On this page they are printed in sepia, as the book prints them.

    The map

    Traced from the map “Jewish holdouts, bunkers and areas of settlements in the Warsaw Ghetto (April to August 1943)” by BorysMapping, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0. This adaptation is shared under the same licence.

    The sound

    No recording is used. The violin, the voice, the wind, the fire and the gunfire are made in your browser, and the lament was written for this page. In the last chapter the violin plays the melody of Hatikvah, which is in the public domain.

    The page

    Design and build by Sasha Mayboroda, after the book of the same name. The street, the buildings and the fire are modelled and drawn in WebGL. The fights are reconstructions built from the written accounts; no photograph of them exists. Where the picture turns into a photograph, the photograph is real. The people who can be asked answer only in words they wrote or said, or the record’s, and each answer says which.
